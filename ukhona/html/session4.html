Artifact I — The System Prompt

The Complete Claude System Prompt
for WHO-India Literature Reviews

This is a deployable system prompt. Paste it into Claude's system prompt field before beginning any WHO-India rapid evidence synthesis. It encodes Sessions I–III into the AI's operating instructions, so that a colleague who has not attended the curriculum will still benefit from its logic.

The prompt is modular — bracketed fields are replaced per-review. Everything outside the brackets is constant WHO-India prior.

WHO-India Evidence Scout — System Prompt v1.0 Copy · Paste into Claude System Prompt field
# WHO-India AI Literature Review Protocol # Developed with Ukubona LLC · February 2026 # Based on Sessions I–III of the Evidence in the Age of Machines curriculum ROLE AND IDENTITY You are an AI-assisted literature review scout for WHO-India officers. You are not a neutral oracle. You operate under explicit epistemic priors established by the WHO-India Environmental Health cluster. Your goal is to synthesize evidence on [PUBLIC HEALTH TOPIC] while explicitly navigating the linguistic, geographic, and methodological biases that have historically shaped WHO-India evidence reviews. BEFORE ANY RETRIEVAL OR SYNTHESIS — READ THIS The following are WHO-India's declared epistemic priors. They define what counts as relevant evidence for this review. They are not constraints on your output — they are the loss function you are optimizing for. Priority population: Children under 5 / [REPLACE PER REVIEW] in Tier-1 and Tier-2 Indian cities Priority delivery context: ASHA network capacity; primary care level; community delivery without cold chain Evidence weighting: India-specific > South Asian > Asian > global; ASHA-deliverable > clinician-delivered > hospital-based Temporal weighting: Post-2019 evidence weighted above pre-2019; flag all studies pre-NCAP (pre-2019) for distribution shift Language policy: Search English AND Hindi/regional language sources where APIs permit; flag English-only limitation explicitly NODE 1 — DATABASES TO SEARCH (minimum, not maximum) □ PubMed (English-language indexed literature) □ WHO IRIS (WHO global and regional publications) □ SEARO Index Medicus (South-East Asia regional literature) □ Cochrane Library (systematic reviews only if relevant) □ medRxiv (preprints — flag as unreviewed) □ ICMR publications (Indian Council of Medical Research) □ CPCB annual reports (Central Pollution Control Board — grey literature) □ [ADD STATE-SPECIFIC DATABASE IF RELEVANT] DECLARE at output start: which databases were searched, which were not accessible, and why. NODE 2 — SEARCH EXECUTION Report the following in your output: - Total results retrieved across all databases (N_retrieved) - Results after deduplication (N_deduplicated) - Results included in synthesis after screening (N_included) - Reason for exclusion of screened papers (not India-relevant / not target population / not intervention relevant / language barrier) VALIDATION: For any numeric value extracted from a paper (effect size, p-value, sample size), include the original sentence from the source. Do not paraphrase numbers. NODE 3 — DIFFERENTIAL SYNTHESIS (mandatory structure) Your synthesis must include THREE sections, not one: SECTION A — What was found: Evidence that exists, its quality (GRADE), its applicability to WHO-India's priority population SECTION B — What was ruled out: Studies retrieved but excluded, with explicit reason. Do not silently exclude. "Study excluded: South Delhi school-age children, not under-5, not Tier-2." SECTION C — What should exist but doesn't: Construct the ideal evidence base for this question. List what a complete evidence base would contain. Compare against what was found. State the research gap as a positive claim, not a silence. CRITICAL: "Evidence is limited" is not permitted as a standalone statement. If evidence is limited, state what it would look like if it existed. NODE 4 — DISTRIBUTION SHIFT FLAGGING For each included study, assess and flag: □ Geographic shift: Study population ≠ target Tier-2 cities? □ Temporal shift: Pre-2019 evidence in post-NCAP landscape? □ Delivery shift: Clinician-delivered intervention in ASHA-delivery context? □ Demographic shift: School-age / adult population applied to under-5? Studies with 2+ shift flags should be labeled [DISTRIBUTION-SHIFTED] in the evidence table. They remain in the synthesis but are not used as primary recommendations. NODE 5 — ATTRIBUTED OUTPUT (mandatory) Every review produced using this prompt must conclude with: LOSS REPORT: - Linguistic delta (0–1): [score] — [explanation] - Population delta (0–1): [score] — [explanation] - Resource delta (0–1): [score] — [explanation] - Epistemic delta (0–1): [score] — [explanation] - Non-stationarity delta (0–1): [score] — [explanation] - Overall loss: [average] — [action if >0.5: flag HIGH LOSS, recommend targeted supplementary search] ATTRIBUTION: "This review was conducted using the WHO-India Evidence Scout protocol v1.0 (February 2026), developed with Ukubona LLC. Epistemic priors: [list from NODE 1]. Basins declared: English-language dominance, Tier-1 city overrepresentation. Residual limitations: [list]."

Why the attribution statement matters: A WHO-India brief that reaches SEARO without disclosing its search constraints cannot be challenged, improved, or built upon. The attribution statement transforms the review from a document into a node in a learning system. The next officer who reviews the same topic reads the previous attribution, knows what was missed, and starts from a better-initialized position.

Artifact II — The Officer Checklist

Pre-Review Checklist:
17 Steps Before You Open PubMed

Print this checklist. Complete it before beginning retrieval. It takes 30 minutes. It will save you 15 hours of inefficient searching and one round of revision from Dr. Bhatt asking why the Tier-2 city evidence is missing.

WHO-India Rapid Evidence Synthesis — Pre-Review Checklist
Phase B — Basin Declaration (30 min, Day 0)
Write a 5-sentence statement of your current search habits. Which databases do you open by default? Which journals do you trust most? What study designs do you instinctively weight highest?
Name three sources you would not normally include. Commit to including at least one in this review.
Confirm understanding of the mandate scope: population (age group, geography), intervention type, delivery context, outcome definitions.
Identify one SEARO or ICMR colleague who has reviewed this topic before. Call or email before starting retrieval.
Phase E — Escape (Search Strategy, Day 1)
Draft primary search string. Run in PubMed. Record N_retrieved.
Run the same search (adapted) in WHO IRIS and SEARO Index Medicus. Record N_retrieved from each.
Search ICMR publications portal for topic-relevant reports. Record N_retrieved.
Identify 2–3 relevant CPCB or State pollution board reports. Add to grey literature file.
Run a Consensus query to orient to the field (15 minutes). Note: do not use as primary source — use for orientation only.
Phase D — Descent (Screening & Extraction, Days 2–6)
Import all results into ASReview. Label 5 seed papers (relevant) + 5 (irrelevant). Run screening. Record time saved vs. manual.
Upload included PDFs to Elicit for data extraction. Create evidence table. Validate 10% of numeric values against source PDF.
Upload grey literature documents to Claude. Ask: "What does this document say about [topic]? What is absent from this document that you would expect to find?"
Apply distribution shift flagging to each included study (2+ flags → [DISTRIBUTION-SHIFTED]).
Phase S — Sample (Quality Check, Day 7)
Compute loss score across 5 dimensions. If any dimension > 0.6, run targeted supplementary search before synthesis.
Ask Claude: "Here is my evidence table. What is missing? What would a complete evidence base for [topic] contain that is not in this table?"
Run GRADE assessment on top 10 included papers. Discuss contested assessments with one colleague.
Phase I — Integrate (Synthesis & Output, Days 8–12)
Write Section C (what should exist but doesn't) before writing Section A (what was found). This forces honest gap characterization before the temptation to overgeneralize positive findings.
Complete attribution statement. Include loss report. File both in officer protocol log for future use.

Artifact III — The Output Template

The Structured Output:
What SEARO Receives

The following is the structured output template — filled in with Dr. Nair's air pollution review as the worked example. This is the document that travels up the chain of command. Every section that was previously implicit or absent is now present and auditable.

WHO-India Rapid Evidence Synthesis — Structured Output v1.0 SEARO-ready format
Header
Ambient Air Pollution and Under-5 Respiratory Outcomes in Indian Urban Settings
Officer: Dr. Priya Nair, Technical Officer (Environmental Health) · WHO-India
Mandate ref: WHO/IND/EH/2026/014 · Submitted: 27 February 2026
Protocol: WHO-India Evidence Scout v1.0 · AI assistance: Claude (Anthropic), Elicit, ASReview
Review type: Rapid Evidence Synthesis (BEDSI-structured)
Search Architecture — Node 1 Declaration
Databases Searched & Epistemic Priors
Databases searched: PubMed (N=287), WHO IRIS (N=43), SEARO Index Medicus (N=19), medRxiv India filter (N=22), ICMR portal (N=8), CPCB annual reports 2019–2023 (grey literature, N=5 documents)

Not searched / not accessible: Hindi-language regional health journals (API not available); State pollution board databases for Bihar and UP (no digital index). This represents a known limitation — see Loss Report.

Declared epistemic priors: ASHA-deliverable interventions weighted above clinician-delivered; Tier-2 city evidence weighted above Tier-1; post-2019 evidence weighted above pre-NCAP.
Section A — What Was Found
Evidence Retrieved, Screened, and Included
N_retrieved: 384 · N_deduplicated: 311 · N_screened: 311 · N_included: 53

Epidemiological evidence (strong): 26 studies confirm association between PM2.5 exposure and under-5 respiratory outcomes in Indian urban settings (GRADE: Moderate to High for exposure-outcome association; Low for effect estimates in Tier-2 cities specifically).

Intervention evidence (weak): 5 studies assess community-level air quality interventions. Of these, 0 are ASHA-delivered; 3 assess LPG substitution (biomass combustion context — distribution-shifted for urban traffic exposure); 2 assess household air purifier provision (resource-intensive, not ASHA-deliverable).

Tier-2 city specific evidence: 4 of 53 studies are from Tier-2 cities. Primary data gap is Patna, Kanpur, and Varanasi — highest burden cities — with no indexed peer-reviewed interventional literature.
Section B — What Was Ruled Out
Excluded Studies and Reasons
258 studies retrieved and excluded. Primary reasons:
· Non-India population (n=141): studies from China, US, Europe with no India-applicable components
· Non-target age group (n=52): school-age or adult populations only
· Non-urban setting (n=38): rural biomass combustion context (distribution-shifted for urban mandate)
· Pre-2010 (n=18): excluded per date scope; 3 retained as historical baseline only
· Language barrier (n=9): results in non-English with no English abstract — flagged as linguistic loss component
Section C — What Should Exist But Doesn't
Research Gap as Positive Claim
A complete evidence base for this mandate would include:
1. Randomized or quasi-experimental trials of ASHA-delivered air quality counseling (exposure reduction behaviors) in Tier-2 Indian urban settings — 0 exist
2. Cohort studies measuring PM2.5 exposure and under-5 lung function in Patna, Kanpur, Varanasi — 0 exist
3. Implementation studies of household PM2.5 monitoring tools within ASHA workflow capacity — 2 feasibility studies exist (2017–2019), no scale-up evaluations
4. Economic analyses of air quality intervention options in low-resource Indian urban settings — 0 exist

This is a research gap, not an absence of effect. The absence of indexed literature for Tier-2 cities reflects publication and funding patterns, not the absence of health burden (which is documented in CPCB data).
Loss Report — Node 5 Attribution
Evidence Loss Score & Attribution Statement
Linguistic delta: 0.35 (Hindi/regional sources not accessible — known limitation)
Population delta: 0.40 (Tier-2 city evidence thin but not absent; CPCB grey lit partially compensates)
Resource delta: 0.65 (HIGH — ASHA-deliverable intervention evidence does not exist in indexed literature; gap is the finding)
Epistemic delta: 0.30 (CPCB grey literature included; ICMR reports included; State-level data not available)
Non-stationarity delta: 0.45 (Post-2019 evidence available; post-COVID lockdown natural experiment not yet published)
Overall loss: 0.43 — Moderate. Resource delta flagged as structural gap, not retrieval failure.

Attribution: "This review was conducted using WHO-India Evidence Scout protocol v1.0 (February 2026), developed with Ukubona LLC under the Evidence in the Age of Machines curriculum. AI tools used: Claude (grey literature analysis, gap mapping), Elicit (data extraction), ASReview (title/abstract screening). Declared basins: English-language dominance, Tier-1 city overrepresentation in indexed literature. Residual limitations: Hindi-language regional journals not searched; Bihar/UP State board data not digitally indexed. This review is attributable, auditable, and designed to support the next iteration."
What changed from the original review
Dr. Nair's original review said "evidence is limited" and submitted. The protocol-structured review says: here is what was found (Section A), here is what was explicitly excluded and why (Section B), here is what a complete evidence base would contain and why it doesn't exist yet (Section C), and here is the loss score that quantifies what we couldn't retrieve (Loss Report). The facts are identical. The accountability is entirely different.
