01
The Core Insight

Why the Tools Alone
Are Not Enough

Session II established an honest ceiling: even with Elicit, ASReview, and Claude working together, Dr. Nair's review would still have suffered from the same structural bias — English-indexed sources, Tier-1 city dominance, invisible grey literature, and contextual judgments made by tools that have no idea what Bihar looks like.

The reason is conceptually precise: all current AI tools optimize for a generic user. Their loss functions — what counts as a good retrieval, a good summary, a useful output — were shaped by RLHF training on millions of interactions with Western, English-speaking, technically sophisticated users. Dr. Nair is not that user. Her territory is different. Her loss function must be different.

Most AI-assisted literature review protocols are designed around the query. You improve the prompt, refine the search terms, chain the tools better. The loss function is implicit — buried in whatever the model's training assumed "good output" means for a generic user. What's needed is to invert this: externalize the loss function itself.

The Ukubona framework — developed across the WHO-India sessions documented at ukb-dt.github.io/who-00 through who-07 — provides a way to make this inversion practical. The core move: put your epistemic priors on a public URL and let the AI read them before it does anything else.

02
The Five Nodes — Applied to Evidence Work

The Pentad:
Language → Science → Art → Life → Meaning

The pentad is not a philosophical abstraction imposed on evidence synthesis. It is a map of the decisions that actually shape every literature review — decisions that are currently being made implicitly, invisibly, by whoever designed the search interface. Making them explicit transforms a search process into a principled search process.

1
Language — The Notation Problem
What can be named as evidence? What databases? What languages? What study types?

Before Dr. Nair types a single search term, she has already made a series of decisions that determine what the review can find. She will search in English. She will use PubMed as her primary database. She will prioritize peer-reviewed journals. She will apply GRADE criteria that weight RCTs above observational studies. These are all Node 1 decisions — they define the notational scheme of the review.

They are not wrong. But they are not neutral. Each one silently filters the corpus before retrieval begins. Naming them is the first act of the framework.

Architecture selection. Before a gradient is computed, you have decided what kind of thing can be wrong. The choice of loss function is a linguistic act. You cannot step outside the frame to evaluate it without another frame.
For WHO-India officers
Your Node 1 declaration: "This review will include English and Hindi-language sources. It will weight ASHA-deliverable evidence above clinician-delivered evidence. It will treat absence of RCT evidence as a research gap, not as absence of effect. It will include CPCB, ICMR, and State pollution board grey literature as Tier-2 evidence."
2
Science — The Formal Machinery
The search, screening, extraction, and GRADE assessment

This is where the tools from Session II operate: PubMed retrieval, ASReview screening, Elicit data extraction, GRADE quality assessment. Node 2 is the domain of the existing workflow — the 100 hours. AI assistance primarily recovers time here.

The critical thing to understand about Node 2 is that it cannot select its own loss function. ASReview learns what you mark as relevant — but what you mark as relevant was already shaped by Node 1 decisions. If your Node 1 silently excluded vernacular sources, ASReview cannot surface them.

Gradient descent. The navigation is rigorous once the landscape is defined. But the algorithm is invisible to the choice of landscape. The map is not the territory, and science alone cannot tell you which map to draw.
3
Art — Differential Synthesis
What was found, what was ruled out, what absences indicate

The narrative synthesis is not data reporting — it is an interpretive act. The medical differential diagnosis analogy is precise: a good synthesizer does not list what they found and stop. They name what was ruled out and why. They identify what should have been there and wasn't. They distinguish between "no evidence" and "evidence not retrieved."

This is the most irreplaceable human contribution to a literature review, and the most undersupported by current AI tools. Claude can assist with structured prompting — but the officer must supply the diagnostic frame.

Regularization and data augmentation. Art manufactures counterfactual evidence — what a complete evidence base would look like — and forces the model to evaluate the current evidence against that ideal baseline. This is the move that separates synthesis from summary.
The Node 3 move in practice
Instead of writing: "Evidence for ASHA-delivered air quality interventions is limited." — write: "A complete evidence base for this question would include: (1) community health worker air quality counseling trials in Indian urban settings; (2) household-level PM2.5 reduction studies in Tier-2 cities; (3) ASHA training protocol evaluations. None of these exist in the indexed literature. This represents a research gap, not an absence of effect."
4
Life — The Indian Terrain
Distribution shift: the evidence was trained on a different territory

The evidence base is non-stationary. India's National Clean Air Programme launched in 2019. COVID lockdowns created a natural experiment in 2020 that changed baseline PM2.5 levels. The ASHA network expanded significantly between 2015 and 2022. Studies conducted before these events may not reflect current terrain.

Additionally: a study from South Delhi is not a study of Kanpur. A study of school-age children is not a study of under-5s. A study funded by a clean cooking initiative is not a study of traffic-related air pollution. These are all distribution shift problems — the evidence was trained on a different territory than the one Dr. Nair is writing about.

This is the space between formal training and deployment — where the model encounters distribution shift, adversarial inputs, regime changes. The weights that worked are suddenly wrong. Not because the algorithm failed but because the territory moved.
For WHO-India officers
Your Node 4 declaration: "Evidence applicability will be assessed against: (a) post-2019 Indian policy landscape; (b) Tier-2 city demographic profiles; (c) ASHA delivery capacity constraints; (d) household income levels in target districts. Studies failing these criteria will be flagged as distribution-shifted, not excluded."
5
Meaning — The Attributed Output
The review knows its own loss function — and declares it

The review that reaches SEARO should not just report findings. It should report the conditions under which those findings were produced. This is what "attribution" means in the framework: not just citing sources, but disclosing the epistemic priors that shaped what sources were searched for, weighted, and included.

This is not a philosophical nicety. It is quality assurance. A SEARO technical advisor reading the brief needs to know whether the review searched SEARO Index Medicus. Whether it weighted grey literature. Whether the Tier-2 city gap is a retrieval artifact or a genuine knowledge gap. A review that attributes its own loss function is a review that can be challenged, improved, and built upon.

Self-supervised learning — the system generates its own training signal, constructs its own notion of what counts as error. This is what genuine expert review looks like: not just optimization, but authorship of the objective.

03
The Kinematic — Motion Through the Landscape

BEDSI: How Learning
Actually Moves

The pentad describes the structure of the landscape. BEDSI — Basin, Escape, Descent, Sample, Integrate — describes the motion through it. Every WHO-India officer arrives at a literature review already occupying a local minimum: habits of search, trusted databases, familiar journals, comfortable analytical categories. BEDSI names the sequence that transforms this starting position into a genuinely better review.

B Basin
Acknowledge where you are. What databases do you habitually use? What sources do you instinctively trust? Name them.
E Escape
Introduce deliberate perturbation. Add one non-PubMed database. Search one non-English source. Read one grey literature document you would normally skip.
D Descent
Follow the new gradient. Use Elicit, ASReview, and Claude with WHO-India priors injected. This is the systematic work phase.
S Sample
Before committing, sample the terrain. Does the new minimum look better? Compute your loss score. Is evidence loss above 0.5? Continue sampling.
I Integrate
The new minimum becomes prior. Update your officer protocol. The next review starts from a better initialized position.

The sequence is recursive. Each integration creates a new basin — which requires a new escape for the next review. This is what distinguishes a learning institution from one that merely produces reports. The first review's Integrate step becomes the next review's Basin. WHO-India's evidence culture improves iteratively, not only from external training, but from its own accumulated practice.

The Diagnostic Instrument

The Evidence Loss Score:
Making the Invisible Visible

The framework's most operationally useful artifact is the evidence loss score — a structured self-assessment that Dr. Nair runs on her evidence base at the Sample step. It does not produce a definitive number. It produces a conversation with the evidence base about its own limitations.

Improvement over Session I baseline (0.58). Resource delta remains high — structural gap in ASHA intervention evidence. Flagged in review as research agenda priority. Linguistic and epistemic deltas reduced by adding SEARO Index Medicus and CPCB grey literature.

The loss score is not a grade. A review with a 0.65 resource delta that explicitly names the gap is better science than a review with a 0.3 resource delta that found interventions by restricting to settings where interventions exist. The score makes the terrain honest.

The counterintuitive principle: a higher loss score, if accompanied by a precise gap characterization, is more valuable to SEARO than a lower loss score achieved by excluding inconvenient terrain. The goal is not to reduce loss by narrowing scope. It is to reduce loss by expanding retrieval, and to name residual loss accurately when it cannot be reduced.
