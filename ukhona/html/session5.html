01
The Institutional Problem

Why Individual Competence
Is Not Enough

Sessions I through IV have equipped Dr. Nair with a framework, a toolset, a protocol, and three deployable artifacts. She is now, by any measure, a more effective evidence synthesizer. Her next review will be better.

But here is the institutional vulnerability: Dr. Nair will eventually be transferred, promoted, or reassigned. The next technical officer who sits at her desk will open PubMed, start from zero, and replicate the same 17-day slog with the same 5 structural failures — unless the institution has done something to hold the learning that Dr. Nair now carries.

An institution that trains individuals and then lets that training evaporate with each personnel change is not a learning institution. It is a training institution — and training institutions are expensive, not cumulative.

What WHO-India needs is a mechanism for converting individual learning into institutional prior — so that the next officer's starting position is better than the current one's, not because they attended a curriculum, but because the institution holds the accumulated intelligence of everyone who came before them.

This is the meaning of Node 5 in the pentad: not the end of the review, but the moment the review becomes infrastructure for the next one.

The Memory Mechanism

The Officer Evidence Profile:
Institutional Memory as Public Infrastructure

The core mechanism is simple: every WHO-India technical officer maintains a public-facing evidence profile — a short, structured document posted at a stable URL that declares their epistemic priors, search protocols, known blindspots, and review history. When they use Claude or any AI tool on a future review, the system prompt instructs the AI to read this URL first.

The profile is not a CV. It is a declaration of methodology. It updates after each review. It is readable by SEARO, by colleagues, by the next officer who inherits the desk, and by the AI tools that will serve that officer.

The profile is the institution's memory. When Dr. Nair's successor reads the profile and sees "Next officer note: Search SEARO Index Medicus first — surfaced 19 papers missed by PubMed," they will search SEARO Index Medicus first. The Implementation Pathway

12-Month Roadmap:
From Pilot to Institution

The following roadmap converts this curriculum into an institutionalized WHO-India practice over 12 months. It assumes no external funding, uses existing staff capacity, and builds on tools that are already free or low-cost.

Month 1–2
Pilot Phase
Proof of Concept
Two officers, one review each, full protocol
Dr. Nair and one colleague run the next scheduled review using the full WHO-India Evidence Scout protocol. Both complete the checklist, use the system prompt, produce the structured output with loss report. Compare time, coverage, and SEARO feedback against a matched historical review.
Two completed reviews with loss reports · Comparison data · Both officers create evidence profiles
Month 3–4
Calibration
Refinement
Loss score calibration and prompt iteration
After two pilot reviews, the protocol will need refinement. Which checklist items consumed time without value? Which Claude prompts produced unhelpful outputs? Which database additions recovered significant evidence vs. minimal evidence? Revise system prompt to v1.1 based on empirical experience.
System prompt v1.1 · Revised checklist · Calibrated loss score thresholds for WHO-India context
Month 5–6
Expansion
Team Adoption
All Environmental Health cluster officers onboarded
Run a 3-hour internal session (using this curriculum as the content) for the full EH cluster. Each officer creates an evidence profile. The AI Toolbox is established: shared access to Elicit and Claude through WHO procurement, ASReview installed on cluster workstations.
Evidence profiles for all EH officers · WHO-India AI toolbox established · Internal training session documented
Month 7–9
SEARO Integration
Regional Scaling
Present to SEARO technical advisory; offer curriculum to regional peers
Share pilot results at a SEARO technical meeting. Three data points: time recovered per review, evidence coverage improvement (loss score reduction), SEARO reviewer feedback on structured output quality. Offer to host a SEARO-wide version of Session I–IV for officers from Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar.
SEARO presentation deck · Pilot data report · Offer letter for regional curriculum
Month 10–12
Institutionalization
Permanent Infrastructure
Protocol embedded in WHO-India SOP; Level 2 curriculum launched
The WHO-India Evidence Scout protocol is adopted as a Standard Operating Procedure for all rapid evidence syntheses. New officer onboarding includes a 2-hour protocol orientation. Level 2 curriculum launched for officers who have completed Level 1 — deeper dives into specific tool use, advanced prompt engineering, multilingual retrieval.

The Curriculum Architecture

Level 2 and Beyond:
The Recursive Structure

Level 1 established the foundations. Each of its five sessions becomes the entry point for a Level 2 deep dive — a more technical, more specific, more demanding treatment of the same terrain. An officer who has completed Level 1 can enter any Level 2 session corresponding to their current need without needing to complete the others first.

Level 1
Foundations
Sessions I–V. The baseline, the tools, the framework, the protocol, the institution. 5 sessions × ~45 min reading. This document.
Level 2
Deepening
One deep session per Level 1 topic. 5 sessions × ~90 min. Technical specifics, worked examples, edge cases, alternative tools, failure modes.
Level 3+
Specialization
Topic-specific curricula: environmental health evidence, infectious disease surveillance, non-communicable disease, maternal and child health. Officers select by cluster mandate.

Coda — What Ukubona Means Here
"To see is to heal. But first: to see the limits of seeing."
The curriculum has not given Dr. Nair more time. It has not given her better databases. It has not found evidence that doesn't exist. What it has done is make the limits of her seeing visible — to herself, to Dr. Bhatt, to SEARO, and to the AI tools that serve her. A review that says "evidence is limited" has seen something and stopped. A review that says "here is what a complete evidence base would contain, and here is precisely what is missing from it" has seen something and named it — which is the beginning of the knowledge that will eventually fill the gap.

The ASHA-delivered urban air quality intervention literature that Dr. Nair could not find in February 2026 does not yet exist. But the funding proposal that will create it — if it is written — will cite Dr. Nair's Section C. The research gap she named is now in the SEARO technical record. The territory has not changed. The map has become more honest.

That is what the curriculum was for.
